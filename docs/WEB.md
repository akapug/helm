# helm web — the browser surface

`helm web` serves the whole station as one self-contained page — no CDN, no
external assets, Python stdlib only. Its source is split by owner-visible
section under `helm/web_ui/`; `manifest.txt` fixes the global CSS → markup → JS
order, and `helm.web_ui_loader` joins the raw bytes on every request without
inventing a separator. The browser still receives one document, one `<style>`
scope, and one classic `<script>` scope. It is a projection of what the CLI
already answers: every view has a terminal equivalent, and the small set of
browser mutations maps one-to-one onto CLI verbs.

```console
$ helm web            # http://127.0.0.1:7433
$ helm web --port 8080 --open
```

## The four pages

**Home** opens at `/` (task/3445 L3): one screen of headline tiles, and the brand (⎈ helm) is the way back to it from any page. Then four top-level pages: **Work · Chat · Fleet · History**. Work and Fleet have section rows: Work's are projects, work, pipeline and backlog (task/3445, task/3643): projects is its own screen on `#work/projects`, and work is the ONE Work page on `#work/work`, with pipeline and backlog its two pre-filtered entries on `#work/pipeline` (the stages under way) and `#work/backlog` (To do, as a list); Fleet's are credit, models (task/3448), seats, sessions, configs and boxes. Chat and History are one page each. Every retired view id still lands on the page that took it over — `#work`, `#board`, `#helm` → Work › projects; `#scheduler` → Work › pipeline, grouped by who has it; `#ledger` → History; `#storage` → boxes.

**Every section change and every link lands on its target** (task/3475), the target's top 8 px below the sticky navigation, measured from the navigation's real height (it wraps at narrow widths): a page with no target of its own lands at its top, Work › projects with a project open lands on that project (again once the page has grown, when the first landing was cut short, until the reader scrolls), and an in-page target (the Work page's stage row and an open card's drawer) scrolls clear of the navigation rather than under it. An open project's line and tabs stick under the navigation; a tab picked from deep inside a long tab lands the project's row, and a target inside the row (the Work tab's page) clears the sticky head as well.

### Project teams (task/3156)

A project's **light** is permission; a family's **burn flag** is supply,
measured once for the fleet. Each project's **team** is the third part,
allocation: its seats, each with a family and a role, and the share of each
short family the project may spend. `helm team` is the verb; each project's
**Team** tab on Work › projects is the owner's door:

- **The Families card** (the head of Fleet › credit) opens on a strip of family chips (the reading's cause on
  hover; a tap selects one) and the selected family's **sheet**: its colour,
  axis and when it changes; for codex, the rate the pool lasts to the next Pro
  reset against the rate the fleet burns; a **stacked bar of every project's
  share** of that family with each share's budget per hour (unallocated shares
  are held back; shares over 100 are flagged over-promised and scale every
  budget down); the **reset credits** in hand and how many accounts were not
  asked for a balance (a credit lifts a rate-limit wall and a workspace
  credits-depleted wall alike); and the
  **declare** control, worse-only, posting to `/api/burn/declare`. A **local
  family's sheet** (qwen27, qwenlocal, any lane on the operator's own GPUs)
  is measured in LANES instead: its recorded capacity, who measured it and
  how (`helm team capacity`), then a **slots bar** — every project's slot
  share of that capacity with the lanes it holds drawn inside it, a segment
  past its slots ringed, the lanes held by a project with no share, the rows
  on no project, and the lanes free. With no capacity recorded it says
  "capacity not measured" and prints no slot count.
- **A project's line** carries its team state and share marks: each
  family its authored share rations, in the project's own colour and ratio
  (`codex RED 2.4×`), each local family whose next row would queue
  (`qwen27 ORANGE 2 of 1.2 slots, queued`), or `team proposed` for a project
  nobody has accepted.
- **The Team tab** carries the **team** right under the light setter: a proposed
  team says so and offers *accept this team*; **budgets**, one row per family
  the team uses, with − and + on the share for a measured family, the budget
  and burn per hour, a bar with the budget marked, and the project's colour
  beside the family's — for a local family, − and + on its slot share, the
  slots it buys, the lanes this project holds against them (inside YELLOW,
  over ORANGE) and QUEUED when one more row would wait for a lane; a family with no share on this project and no present seat folds behind one
  **N other families** line, and absent seats behind **N absent** (task/3475;
  one click opens either, and it stays open across the redraws); the
  **members**, each with its state (live, quiet,
  absent, wanted), a role select, remove, and a *shared* badge for a seat on
  more than one team (its spend is billed to no project); an **add seat** row
  (a seat running now, or a new `<project>-<family>` seat for the lead to
  start); **what the lead will do** (the drift list, each row's `say` where
  its lead's line names a verb); **what agents hear**, a
  preview of `helm route <kind> --project <key>` for the draft (review,
  build, verify, research or council; it drops a family outside the owner's
  approval tier for a review or a verification, as route does at E3, and
  marks a local lane past its slots QUEUED, never refused); the
  **history** of team writes and light changes; and a **save bar** that shows

Edits stay a draft in the page until saved, and survive the minute's
re-read. A save is compare-and-set on the version the card showed: a team
another tab or a seat moved answers 409, the card says so and keeps the
edits. The server is the authority on every saved number (`teams.board_model`
over the `teams` leg of `/api/board`); the page re-folds a DRAFT with the same
readings and the same rule so a − or + moves the budget, the colour and the
route preview at once. The doors advise: `helm dispatch send` and `helm work
claim` refuse nothing on a share. `helm route`, and the card's preview of it
(*what agents hear*), read the project's own colour, so a family past twice
the project's budget (RED on its share) is DROPPED from the project's answer
(edge E33), as a RED family is for everyone.

## Security posture

- **Loopback only.** The server binds `127.0.0.1` (default port **7433**);
  there is no listen-on-all option. Put a reverse proxy with its own auth in
  front if you ever need remote access — helm itself will not do it.
- **Host/Origin validation.** Every request's `Host` (and `Origin`/`Referer`
  when present) must be a loopback literal for the bound port
  (`127.0.0.1:<port>`, `localhost:<port>`, `[::1]:<port>`) — anything else is
  403. This is the DNS-rebinding defense: a hostile page re-resolved to
  loopback never reads a response.
- **The mutation token.** Every POST demands
  `Authorization: Bearer <MUTATION_TOKEN>` — 403 without. The token is minted
  fresh per process and handed to the UI by template substitution
  (`__HELM_TOKEN__` in the assembled UI source is replaced at serve time; the
  fragments on disk stay raw). `HELM_API_TOKEN` (or a declared predecessor's
  `<NAME>_API_TOKEN`; see [local names](ENVIRONMENT.md#local-names)) pins it —
  set that when a script or a long-lived service needs stable POST access.
  A tab that outlives the process (the unit recycles hourly by design) holds
  a dead token; the UI self-heals on the resulting 403 by re-reading its own
  served page for the live token and retrying the mutation exactly once — a
  genuine forbidden still surfaces loudly.
- **Bounded writes.** POST bodies over 64 KB are rejected; every mutation is
  reversible by construction (renames, moves-to-trash, backups — the
  archive-not-delete law).
- **Graceful degrade.** A subsystem that is absent or misbehaving answers
  `{"unavailable": true}`, never a 500 that takes the page down.

## POST endpoints

All demand the bearer token; bad requests return `400` with a class-only
`error`/`code`, while a stale config save returns `409 conflict`. `web.POST_API`
is the authority on the set; the surfaces below are its owner-facing doors, and
nothing else in the browser may write:

**On the page, every one of them rides `post()`** — the helper that attaches the
per-process bearer, re-reads a stale one and retries once, and raises the
server's own `error` sentence. Its sibling `j()` is the READ helper and takes
`(url, ms)` only; it refuses a request body rather than downgrading it to a GET,
because that downgrade shipped once and cost the work tab its comment button:
the route, the handler and the token were all healthy while the page quietly
asked for a GET at a POST-only path and the owner read `404` in a toast.

## Running it as a service

The deployment that runs live is a systemd **user** unit. Install helm on
your PATH first (`ln -s <repo>/bin/helm ~/.local/bin/helm`), then:

```ini
# ~/.config/systemd/user/helm-web.service
[Unit]
Description=helm web — the personal knowledge home UI
After=default.target

[Service]
ExecStart=%h/.local/bin/helm web --port 7433
Restart=on-failure
RestartSec=5
# the allocator bound (see "Memory" below); `helm web unit --install`
# writes the same two lines as a drop-in, which systemd reads after this file
Environment=MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2
Environment=MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=131072
# optional: pin the mutation token for scripted access
# Environment=HELM_API_TOKEN=<your-token>

[Install]
WantedBy=default.target
```

```console
$ systemctl --user daemon-reload
$ systemctl --user enable --now helm-web
$ systemctl --user status helm-web
```

`loginctl enable-linger $USER` keeps it running when you are not logged in.

### Memory: the allocator bound, the room index, and ad hoc servers

The console used to reach its 2 GB `MemoryHigh` every hour and spill
1.3-1.9 GB more into swap (task/3715). Most of it sat in glibc's per-thread
arena heaps: memory the server had freed and the allocator kept. `helm web`
is a threaded server, and glibc serves a large buffer from a thread's arena
once its dynamic mmap threshold has risen, so a multi-megabyte read freed on a
handler thread stays in that arena. Two variables bound it:

| variable | value | effect |
|---|---|---|
| `MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_` | `131072` | a fixed threshold: every block of 128 KiB or more is its own mapping and goes back to the kernel when freed (a probe of the pattern: 527 MB to 226 MB) |
| `MALLOC_ARENA_MAX` | `2` | at most two arenas, whatever the thread count |

glibc reads them when a process starts, so `helm web` carries them three ways
(`helm/webmem.py`): it puts any the environment lacks into its own
environment, which `os.execv` hands to every re-exec onto a changed tree; it
applies them to the life already running through `mallopt(3)`; and the unit
carries them. `helm web unit` prints the drop-in
(`~/.config/systemd/user/helm-web.service.d/allocator.conf`) and
`helm web unit --install` writes it and reloads the user manager. It restarts
nothing: the unit's next start reads it. An unchanged drop-in is not
rewritten, and the manager is reloaded for it only when it reports it has not
loaded it (`NeedDaemonReload`, as after a run whose reload failed). A value
the operator set in the process environment wins over helm's. In the unit it
does not: systemd reads a drop-in after the unit file, and the later
`Environment=` of one variable wins, so `allocator.conf` replaces a value set
in `helm-web.service` itself. Set your own in a drop-in that sorts after it
(`systemctl --user edit helm-web` writes `override.conf`). Its start line in
the log reads `helm web: allocator bounded (task/3715): MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2,
MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=131072`.

The two ledger reads (`/api/ledger`'s post labels and `/api/ledger/native`'s
chat pulse) no longer re-read every room on every 15 s walk. Each keeps a fold
per room (its answer so far, the byte offset it read to and the file's
identity) and on the next walk reads nothing from a room that did not move,
only the new bytes of a room that grew, and the whole room again after
anything else (a rotation or restore puts a new file in place; a rolled-back
append truncates in place). A room gone from the listing is dropped, and a
room that will not open is named in the answer, never folded into a quieter
count.

Any `helm web` that is not the helm-web unit (a lane's server, a private
board, a live-UI check) runs under no memory limit. `helm doctor` lists each
one on its `ad hoc web servers:` line with its port, pid, RSS (with swap), age
and the tree it serves, and says `none` when there is none; whoever started
one stops it once its work is done.

The server on the console port also runs the **stop-facts leg**: the one
writer of `_global/web-cache/stop-facts.json`, the facts every seat's Stop
hook reads instead of folding the dispatch ledger and asking git per lease
(docs/HOOKS.md, "Stop facts"). It polls its inputs every second, recomputes
only what moved, refreshes everything every minute, and runs the memory-index
cap and scratch reaper as a minute job. The composition-seam facts (the room
census, green receipts and per-room seam rows of every repository a claim or a
rostered seat names) refresh on their own key, per repository, so a slow one
never delays the lease facts. With no `helm web` running, every
claims exemption (a lane in gate, a lane approved) is refused at every stop
and `helm doctor` says so. `HELM_STOP_FACTS_LEG=1|0` decides for a server on
any other port.

**Every `helm web` re-execs itself when its code changes on disk**, on any
port, from a follow thread: once the tree's code digest (every `helm/**/*.py`)
has read the same for a second and the new tree imports in a child
interpreter, it runs its own command line again with `os.execv` (same
interpreter, port and environment; `--open` is dropped, so a land opens no
new tab), within a minute of the land. Its log reads `helm web: the tree
changed; re-exec'ing onto it: ...`. A tree that does not import is not exec'd
onto: the server keeps serving, logs one line saying why, and does not retry
until the tree moves again. No path unit or restart step is needed for a
board, or the stop facts, to follow a land.

On the console port the stop-facts leg is the follow thread's follower, so
the process has one exec path: the exec waits out any snapshot write in
flight and releases the writer lock first. `HELM_WEB_FOLLOW=0` keeps a board
on the code it started with and says so at start; the console ignores it
(its facts are refused once its code is older than the tree) and says that
instead. A process whose command line is not a `helm web`, or one serving
`--port 0`, never re-execs.
